Outdoor Living Ideas, and the Decisions Behind Them

Home / Outdoor Living Ideas, and the Decisions Behind Them

Anyone can show you a gallery. The useful part is why each of these was built that way — because the decision behind an idea is what you actually have to copy.

Finish the Ceiling, Not Just the Roof

The cheapest version of any covered structure leaves the underside of the roof exposed. Finishing that ceiling is the single change that makes a structure read as a room rather than a shelter.

On our West Ashley cabana the ceiling is tongue and groove, painted haint blue, with cedar posts and beams and craftsman trim around them. Tape lighting hidden above the beams washes it at night, which is why the space still works after dark.

It is also the first thing quietly removed from a cheaper quote.

Let the Deck and the Structure Be One Idea

A pavilion dropped onto an existing pool deck always looks added. The Mount Pleasant pool pavilion works because the travertine deck, the structure and the kitchen were designed as one thing, with the paving running through instead of stopping at the posts.

The beams are the other half of it: oversized engineered timbers let us set the rafters 24 inches apart, which is what gives it the grand, timber-framed feel. The homeowners said it felt like something from a high-end resort.

Ideas That Cost Less Than People Expect

  • A bar top with a foot rail. It is what turns a cooking counter into somewhere guests stand. On the Summerville kitchen the rail is sapele, which is worth the upgrade on the one part people touch.
  • A fire pit off the corner of the patio. $1,000 for a kit, from $2,500 custom, and it extends the season by months.
  • Uplighting one tree. About $200 a light plus a transformer, and visible from the street, the porch and inside the house.
  • A fountain in a small courtyard. From $1,000 for a kit. Sound is the one thing a photo cannot sell you.

Ideas Worth Doing Only If You Want Them

A putting green, a very specific pool layout, an outdoor space built around one hobby — these are excellent reasons to build and poor reasons to expect the next owner to pay for it.

The Summerville putting green sits beside a pergola kitchen, and it works because the family wanted it. Build that sort of thing for yourself, and build cover, patio, lighting and planting if resale is on your mind.

The Idea Behind Most Good Backyards

Every project that works is really a set of clear paths between places to cook, sit and swim, with enough space around each for furniture and for people to pass behind it. Fire pits want 6 feet of clearance. Kitchens want somewhere for guests to stand that is not in the cook's way. Walkways want to go where people already walk.

Get that right and almost any feature works. Get it wrong and no amount of material fixes it. Our design guide walks through the order those decisions get made.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is worth spending extra on in an outdoor living space?

A finished ceiling on any covered structure, trim detail, and lighting. Those are the things that make a structure read as a room, and the first things removed from a cheaper quote.

What are the cheapest changes that make the biggest difference?

A fire pit off the corner of the patio, uplighting one good tree, a bar top with a foot rail, and a fountain in a small courtyard.

How much clearance does outdoor furniture need?

Leave 6 feet of clear space around a fire pit for chairs and a walkway behind them, and give a kitchen somewhere for guests to stand out of the cook's way, usually a bar top.
