Building in North Charleston

Most of the North Charleston backyards on this page start with a patio. Ten by ten is the smallest patio we would recommend, and a fire pit needs more, because you need room to pull chairs back. From there people add a pergola for shade, an outdoor kitchen, or lighting so the yard works after dark.

Soil around Charleston runs from fast-draining sand to heavy clay depending on the area, which is one reason every yard needs a look before we price it. If water sits after a storm, we fix the drainage before building anything where it collects. If the lawn is the problem, the choice is usually between new sod on a corrected grade and artificial turf where grass keeps failing.

Roofed structures, taller retaining walls and most gas, electrical or plumbing work usually need a permit, though an outdoor shower does not. We pull the permit ourselves as part of the build.

North Charleston Rules and Neighborhoods

The City of North Charleston allows fences up to 4 feet in a front yard and 8 feet in a side or rear yard, in materials from brick and wood to wrought iron and approved vinyl. If your home is in an HOA, the city asks for proof of HOA approval before it issues a permit. Trees of 10 inches or more are protected, grand trees of 24 inches or more are removed only when there is no alternative, and the city charges $217.50 an inch when a grand tree isn’t replaced.

  • Park Circle (Olde North Charleston): the historic district is Craftsman bungalows, Charleston singles and cottages with deep front porches and picket fences under mature street trees. Fences and retaining walls there must be stone, brick, stucco or wood pickets, front fences stop at 4 feet, and a driveway stays unpaved or is brick or gravel laid as two tire strips. Landscaping and sprinkler work don’t need review.
  • Wescott Plantation: fences are metal or wood, 4 to 6 feet, with no chain link or white vinyl. On golf lots they are black and 4 feet at most. Pergolas, fire pits and outdoor kitchens are allowed with approval, and up to ten small low-voltage lights need no request at all.
  • Indigo Fields: no chain link, front yards must be sodded, and retaining walls are brick or stucco.

Rules as published when we checked in 2026. Guidelines change, and the board or town always has the final say, so we confirm the current rules for your property before a design is final.

Every Service We Offer, Available In North Charleston

The projects on this page are only part of what we do. We offer all of our services in North Charleston, designed by Doug and Zach and built by our own crew. From the first site visit to the final walkthrough, here’s how a project works.

  • Landscaping: landscape design and installation, plants and trees, sod, artificial turf, irrigation, drainage and grading, landscape lighting, mulch and bed edging, fountains and water features
  • Hardscaping: patios and pavers, concrete and driveways, retaining walls, fire pits, pool decks
  • Outdoor Structures: pergolas, pavilions, outdoor kitchens, outdoor fireplaces, outdoor showers

Worth knowing before you start

  • Sod: A new lawn is only as good as the grade underneath it, and we never lay sod over old grass. Sod beats seed here: downpours wash seed away and humidity breeds fungus.
  • Artificial turf: It earns its money where grass keeps failing: deep shade, a dog run, a narrow side yard, or under a structure. It runs hotter than grass in full sun, and it lasts 15 to 25 years.
  • Pool decks: A new concrete pool deck is about $12 per square foot. Pavers, porcelain, travertine and stone start about $18. Resurfacing is quoted on condition. We resurface with cool deck or microcement, or lay tile over the existing slab. Cool deck is the most affordable. A coating over a deck that is still moving will fail, because the problem is underneath it.
  • Plants and trees: Hedges are a better answer for screening than a taller fence. They read as landscape and don’t need a variance.

Small jobs are welcome too. There’s no minimum job size.
