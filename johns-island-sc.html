Johns Island Landscaping, Designed to Look Like It Belongs

Cramers Landscaping is a family company. Doug Cramer has spent more than 30 years building landscapes in the Lowcountry, and his son Zach is our licensed residential builder. Doug and Zach design every project themselves, and our own crew builds it.

The Johns Island project on this page is a Lowcountry woodland garden, with an entrance pergola, bluestone walkways and planting.

A Lowcountry Woodland Garden

The feel this client wanted was a Lowcountry woodland garden.

You enter through a pergola built from pressure-treated lumber and stained. From there, bluestone walkways lead through the planting: mondo grass as ground cover, Japanese maples, ferns and tractor seat plant.

A garden like this is mostly plants, and on a large property the landscaping is not the cheap part. A full design and installation can cost as much as the hardscape beside it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of work have you done on Johns Island?

The project on this page is a Lowcountry woodland garden: an entrance pergola in stained pressure-treated lumber, bluestone walkways, and planting with mondo grass, Japanese maples, ferns and tractor seat plant.

What is an entrance pergola built from?

This one is pressure-treated lumber, stained. Pressure-treated pine is the most cost-effective option and performs well with proper finishing.

How much does planting cost?

Plant beds run $1,000 to $2,000, a front yard is $5,000 to $10,000, and a full house landscape is $20,000 or more in plants. Plant size is a real budget lever: buying smaller and waiting two seasons is a legitimate way to hit a number.

Do you also work on Kiawah Island?

Yes, we have installed a new-construction landscape on Kiawah Island. For farther areas like Kiawah there is a trip charge for the consultation.
