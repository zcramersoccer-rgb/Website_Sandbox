Outdoor Showers Rinse Off Before You Get in the Door

An outdoor shower is one of those things people put off and then wonder why they waited. Sand from Folly or the Isle of Palms stays outside. The dog gets rinsed before the rug does. After a day in the yard you are clean before you reach the back door.

We build the enclosure, run the hot and cold water and handle the drainage ourselves. The plumbing is done in-house by our own crew, the same as the gas and electrical on our kitchens and pavilions, so there is no waiting on a second trade to show up and finish someone else’s work.

Doug and Zach Cramer look at every shower themselves before quoting it, across Charleston, Mount Pleasant, Sullivan’s Island, Isle of Palms, Folly Beach and Summerville.

What an Outdoor Shower Costs

Outdoor showers run from $6,000 to $15,000. Where a given shower falls depends on three things: how big it is, what the enclosure is built from, and what you stand on.

Outdoor shower, installed$6,000 – $15,000
Pressure-treated enclosurethe budget end of that range
Sapele hardwood enclosurethe top of the range

Ranges come from our own completed showers. The hardscape you stand on and the shower fixture you choose also move the number. Every quote comes after we see the spot.

The fixture is worth spending a little on. It is outside in salt air, it gets used wet and it is the one part of the shower you touch every single time.

Pressure-Treated or Sapele

We build enclosures from pressure-treated lumber or from sapele. Pressure-treated is the affordable option and it works. Sapele is a dense hardwood that costs considerably more and holds up better than anything else we use out here.

The honest answer on materials: any of them survive with proper maintenance, and none of them survive neglect. If you want the longest life with the least fuss, that is sapele. It is the same wood we used for the foot rail on the Summerville outdoor kitchen.

Hot and Cold, Not Cold Only

We plumb outdoor showers with hot and cold water. A cold-only shower gets used in August and then sits unused for eight months of the year. With hot water it stays useful in March and October, and it is a far better rinse after the beach.

Where the Water Goes

Most of our outdoor showers drain into a dry well. The water soaks away below grade instead of running across the yard, pooling against the house or killing the grass beside the slab. Getting that right matters more here than most places, since our water table is high and a shower that drains badly turns into a permanent wet spot.

Permits and HOA Approval

An outdoor shower does not require a permit. What it does sometimes require is HOA approval, particularly when the shower will be visible from the street. If your neighborhood has an architectural review, we will prepare and submit that application for you, the same as we do for pavilions and kitchens.

How We Build Yours

  1. We look at the spot. Free consultation at your home. Where the water line runs, where the sun hits, what the neighbors can see, and where the water will go.
  2. You pick the materials. Pressure-treated or sapele for the enclosure, the hardscape underfoot and the fixture.
  3. HOA paperwork, if you need it. We prepare and submit the application.
  4. We plumb it. Hot and cold, run by our own crew, with the dry well set for drainage.
  5. We build the enclosure and finish the surface you stand on.

Outdoor showers pair naturally with the rest of a backyard project. Ours most often go in alongside a pool pavilion, a paver patio or a pool deck.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an outdoor shower cost in Charleston?

Ours run from $6,000 to $15,000 installed. Size and material drive the number: a pressure-treated enclosure sits at the affordable end and sapele hardwood at the top. The hardscape underfoot and the shower fixture also move it.

Should an outdoor shower have hot water?

Yes. We plumb hot and cold rather than cold only. A cold-only shower gets used in high summer and ignored the rest of the year, while hot water keeps it useful in spring and fall and makes it a much better rinse after the beach.

How does an outdoor shower drain?

Most of our outdoor showers drain to a dry well, so the water soaks away below grade instead of running across the yard or pooling against the house. That matters here, where the water table is high.

Do you need a permit for an outdoor shower in Charleston?

No permit is required. Some HOAs do want approval, especially if the shower is visible from the street, and we will prepare and submit that application for you.

What is the best wood for an outdoor shower on the coast?

Any material lasts with proper maintenance, but hardwoods hold up best. We build enclosures from pressure-treated lumber or sapele, and sapele offers the best longevity of anything we use.
